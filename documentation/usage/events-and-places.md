# Events and places

Events and places link people and content together and put them in order: who was where, and when.

## Events

An event is something that happened: a **birth**, **marriage**, **divorce**, **death** or **migration**, an **other** event with its own label - or a **historical** event like an invasion or a transfer of sovereignty, which happened to everyone at once.

An event has a date (exact, *ca.*, *before* or *after*, or just a year), places, the people involved, and the photos and documents that record it - a birth announcement for a birth, a passenger list for a migration.

- Births and deaths are added on a person's page (see [People](people.md)).
- Other events: on a person's page, or from a photo or document in edit mode - **+ new event** starts on the item's date, which you can change: a clipping is usually published just after the event.
- **Who was there:** started from a person's page, the new event is theirs, and you can press their partners, parents and children to add them too - and pick anyone else. More people are added on the event's own page.
- **A marriage or divorce** is between partners: someone you add who isn't linked as a partner yet becomes one when you save.
- Each event has its own page, with its people, places, documents and comments.
- **Proof:** in edit mode, *documented by* shows the photos and documents the event's people are in - closest in date first - to link with one click. Anything else, and for an event without people: search all content below it.

## History and the calendar

*More* → **Events** lists the historical events - the history the family lived through - and is where new ones are added. They also appear in the timeline of everyone alive at the time. Someone linked to a historical event - they took part in it - has it as their own event; for everyone else it stays history.

**Background.** Some history is context for one particular event rather than for everyone: "Sibajak released for repatriation" matters to those who arrived on the Sibajak later, though they weren't part of it. On the page of the event with the people - the arrival - in edit mode, add it under **Background**, or make it right there with **+ new historical event**. Then:

- the people of that event see it on their timeline, marked *background to …* - also when it happened before they were born;
- it no longer shows as history for everyone else - linked, it's specific context;
- it only works one way: being in the background event doesn't bring the event it explains.

Background is for the people in an event, so only an event with people has it. To say that history led to other history - the atomic bombs to the surrender of Japan - use the description: both stay history for everyone. The background event's page lists, read only, what it's background for.

The **calendar** shows the whole year: every birth, marriage, death and historical event by day, with how long ago it was.

## Places

*More* → **Places** lists the places in the archive, each within its larger place: *Batavia* within *Java* within *Nederlands-Indië*. A place's page shows what happened there: the people born or died there, the events, and the photos and documents.

Places are added where they're needed - while describing a photo or an event, type a new name and it's created. In edit mode a place's page lets you set its larger place, so places that came in loose can be put where they belong.

## Finding news around a person

Because events, places and documents are linked, a person's page and timeline show the history around them - and a place's page shows who else lived there. That's where new leads often start.
