# Research

An archive raises questions: who's on this photo, how did this clipping come about, where did they live in 1946? FMLY3 keeps the research next to what it's about.

## Notes

A note is a piece of research, in your own words. Each note has a kind, so open work stands out:

- **To do** and **question** - what's still open
- **Hypothesis** - an idea to test
- **Conclusion** - what you found out
- **Context** - background that helps understand the rest
- **Scratch** - anything else

A question can grow into a hypothesis and a conclusion as you find out more: change its kind as you go.

Link a note to the **people**, **photos and documents**, **events** and **places** it's about - it then shows on their pages too. Make a note from the item or person itself (*new note* on its page), and it's linked straight away.

A new note is yours until you share it. *More* → **Notes** lists all notes you may see, open work first.

**Tip:** a research line about a photo or a person belongs in a note, not in a comment or a tag - a note has room for the question and the answers, and it stays findable as open work.

## Mentioning people, items, events and places

In a biography, a description, a note or a comment, a mention links to what you refer to: `[Eric](person:…)`, `[the passenger list](content:…)`, `[the wedding](event:…)`, `[Soerabaja](place:…)`, `[the question](note:…)` - after the colon the code from its address (`/person/aa7d8ab2…/` → `person:aa7d8ab2…`). Everyone sees it as a link only if they may see what it points to; anyone else sees the text - a person's name as initials. Leave the label empty - `[](event:…)` - and its own name is filled in when it's shown: always current, and as each reader may see it. **Type `@` and a few letters** - or several words, "@eric coomans", or a code from an address - to choose instead: people, items, events, places and notes you may see appear under the field, by kind - arrows and Enter (or a click) put the mention in, Escape closes the list. **Your own words as the link:** type them in brackets with `(@` after - `[the mother of their children](@riedé` - and choose: only the link's address is filled in, your words stay.

**Mentioned in.** A person's, item's, event's, place's or note's page lists where it's mentioned - in a biography, a description, a note, a comment or a transcript - as far as you may read those: a private note or a comment on something hidden doesn't show. Under each, the words around the mention, so you see what's said without opening it. It appears once there's something to list. **Not everyone sees every link.** Choosing what to mention, a pill says when not everyone may see it - *family only*, *private*, *draft*; after saving, a message says so once when you've mentioned something fewer people may see than the text you wrote it in. Those readers see only the words. In edit mode, *remove link* next to a text you may change drops the link and keeps the words. Revoking or deleting something that's mentioned says so first - those links would lead nowhere for most readers - and points to *Housekeeping → mentions that lead nowhere* after.

## Search

The search field in the header searches the whole archive at once: people, photos and documents, events, notes, places and tags - including the text of transcripts and biographies. Results appear while you type, a section per kind. When a word is found in a text rather than a name, the words around it are shown, so you can see why it matched.

## Comments

Below a photo, a person or an event: add a memory, a correction, or ask the family what they know. Everyone who can see the item can read the conversation and join in. **Who may read a comment** shows on it: it takes its item's visibility - family-only on a family photo - and may be narrower (a private aside), never wider; choose it under the comment when writing, or later in edit mode (yours, or as staff). An event has no visibility of its own: its comments start as community. Changing an item's visibility later changes no comment by itself. Made narrower, a notice under its visibility says how many comments are now more visible than the item - *narrow them* with one click, or leave them: they're listed under the loose end *comments wider than their item*: was the item narrowed on purpose? If not, restore the item; if so, *narrow* the comment - or leave it, it isn't shown wider than its item anyway. Made wider, a notice offers to *widen* its narrower comments: your own - who reads your comment is your choice - or, for staff, all of them (or one by one, on the comments: a narrower one may be meant for fewer readers). *More* → **Comments** shows all recent comments on what you may see; the latest are also on the dashboard. Comments on a revoked item stay on its own page, out of the list.

You can change or remove your own comments.

When staff revoke a photo or document, they're asked why: the reason is kept as a comment titled *Revocation reason* on the item - who revoked it and why, also after it's restored.

## Marking a loose end

Found something that needs work later? Give it the tag **Loose end**: it appears under *marked as a loose end* on the dashboard until you remove the tag.
