# Research

An archive raises questions: who's on this photo, how did this clipping come about, where did they live in 1946? FMLY3 keeps the research next to what it's about.

## Notes

A note is a piece of research, in your own words. Each note has a kind, so open work stands out:

- **To do** and **question** - what's still open
- **Hypothesis** - an idea to test
- **Conclusion** - what you found out
- **Context** - background that helps understand the rest
- **Scratch** - anything else

A question can grow into a hypothesis and a conclusion as you find out more: change its kind as you go.

Link a note to the **people**, **photos and documents**, **events** and **places** it's about - it then shows on their pages too. Make a note from the item or person itself (*new note* on its page), and it's linked straight away.

A new note is yours until you share it. *More* → **Notes** lists all notes you may see, open work first.

**Tip:** a research line about a photo or a person belongs in a note, not in a comment or a tag - a note has room for the question and the answers, and it stays findable as open work.

## Search

The search field in the header searches the whole archive at once: people, photos and documents, events, notes, places and tags - including the text of transcripts and biographies. Results appear while you type, a section per kind. When a word is found in a text rather than a name, the words around it are shown, so you can see why it matched.

## Comments

Below a photo, a person or an event: add a memory, a correction, or ask the family what they know. Everyone who can see the item can read the conversation and join in. *More* → **Comments** shows all recent comments on what you may see; the latest are also on the dashboard. Comments on a revoked item stay on its own page, out of the list.

You can change or remove your own comments.

When staff revoke a photo or document, they're asked why: the reason is kept as a comment titled *Revocation reason* on the item - who revoked it and why, also after it's restored.

## Marking a loose end

Found something that needs work later? Give it the tag **Loose end**: it appears under *marked as a loose end* on the dashboard until you remove the tag.
